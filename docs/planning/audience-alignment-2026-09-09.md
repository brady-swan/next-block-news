## The opportunity

The knowledge base changes my understanding of the product more than it changes my list
of suitable topics. NBN should help Bitcoiners stay oriented to consequential changes in
money, ownership, freedom, and the technology and institutions that shape those things.
Bitcoin is the center of gravity, not a keyword every qualifying story must contain.

This audience includes savers, builders, people protecting themselves from financial
repression, institutional participants, and people interested in open systems. Their
interests overlap, but they are not one ideological faction. Covering them well means
understanding their questions, not assuming they all hold the same answers.

My recommendation is to improve our editorial understanding first, then use it to direct
discovery and follow-through. A larger source roster alone would leave the deeper issue
untouched. Equally, this should not become a general politics or technology publication.

## 1. Give every editorial seat the same audience understanding

The writer brief already permits useful macro, actual Bitcoin use, and substantive
culture. However, the intake mailroom still lists mostly Bitcoin/monetary categories,
and assignment preparation explicitly describes its scope as “Bitcoin or monetary.”
Privacy, financial freedom, and related AI are not comparably explicit upstream.

That is a plausible blind spot, not a measured claim that those filters have rejected
a particular number of good stories. We should test it against actual examples.

Proposed changes:

- Replace and consolidate the audience/scope sections rather than append another long
  essay. Use a compact shared audience brief, with short role-specific instructions for
  intake, preparation, writer, editor, and audit. Check the active payload at each seat.
- Explain why purchasing power, access to money, private ownership, censorship,
  self-custody, open technology, energy, and institutional power matter to these readers.
- Treat AI as relevant when a development concretely affects those concerns: privacy,
  surveillance, security, access, money, or energy. A generic model launch is not enough.
- Keep established boundaries: no routine software releases, trivial treasury buys,
  trading signals, generic altcoin promotion, or minor macro ticks. A meaningful release
  in a larger security/governance story remains eligible.
- Teach that readers are knowledgeable, not omniscient. Explain an unfamiliar product
  or metric briefly; do not explain Bitcoin from scratch or turn every post into a lesson.
- Preserve disagreement and disconfirming evidence. The audience lens guides relevance;
  it must not predetermine the reported conclusion.

There should be no new topic quota or deterministic relevance gate. These are judgment
instructions and examples, not an expanded keyword allowlist.

## 2. Give the newsroom questions to pursue, not only tips to process

The most promising new capability is a small reporting agenda drawn from existing
storylines and their `watch_for` notes. We already store these, alongside notebooks,
unresolved questions, research artifacts, and publishing state. Do not rebuild memory.

A useful active question would name:

1. What we know and when it was true.
2. What remains uncertain or contested.
3. What observable change would be worth reporting.
4. Where we would most likely see that change first.

Illustrative questions, not claims about current news:

- A monetary official signals intervention. Was a concrete measure subsequently announced,
  rejected, or implemented?
- A proposed rule threatens self-custody. What does the actual text cover, and did a court
  or regulator subsequently change the outcome?
- A Bitcoin initiative announces an ambitious adoption goal. Are people actually using it,
  and what problem is it solving for them?
- An AI/mining story claims competition for power. Is there an actual facility, contract,
  electricity-price change, or operating decision behind that claim?

Begin with a few owner-reviewed questions in existing storylines and audit-assisted
follow-up. A later, explicitly approved step could let these direct bounded Perception
or primary-source checks when a fresh cue or expected milestone arrives. Do not research
every open question every 15 minutes. Retire resolved questions and record negative
outcomes as readily as confirmations.

This creates a path toward finding the next development before a guide account packages
it, while continuing to learn from those accounts. It does not require an immortal agent
or a second newsroom.

## 3. Make reader understanding the value we add

We have sometimes treated a source summary as the finished product. The stronger question
is: what does a reader understand after our post that was hard to see in the source?

That value can be small and fast:

- Put the consequence ahead of the institutional announcement.
- Distinguish a proposal from an enacted rule, a transfer from a proven sale, or revenue
  from profit when the distinction changes the story.
- Show the size and comparison that make a number meaningful, without adding a statistics
  dump or an entire history of the topic.
- Explain the unfamiliar term that otherwise makes a good story inaccessible.
- Clarify what changed since our last post instead of repeating its background.

Use the established short sentences and one- or two-sentence paragraphs. Do not force a
“this matters for Bitcoin because…” ending. The relevance often belongs in selection,
not in an ideological conclusion attached to the copy.

Crucially, added value is not another publishing hurdle. A clear, timely, well-sourced
report is already useful. We should not abandon a good story because we cannot add an
exclusive angle, original analysis, or a custom image.

## 4. Cover lived outcomes, including things that work

An audience shaped by the KB is interested in who can save, transact, build, and retain
control—not just which institution announced something. Our inputs should let us see
those outcomes outside the US corporate-finance news cycle.

This includes actual use under inflation or capital controls, financial access restored
or removed, meaningful privacy victories and losses, and credible evidence that open
technology is solving a problem. It also includes evidence that an advertised solution
did not work. Neither outrage nor promotional optimism should be our default frame.

This is not a requirement to add a daily human-interest story. It is a deliberate effort
to make such developments visible and recognizable when there is real news.

## 5. Build an information network with different jobs

Our five protected guide accounts remain useful: Bitcoin News, Bitcoin Archive,
Bitcoin Magazine, TFTC, and Simply Bitcoin. A new expert account should not automatically
inherit the same every-post research treatment.
The owner explicitly approved adding @KobeissiLetter to the guide group in the next build;
it is already collected as Tier 2 research/data. Its attention role and evidence tier remain
separate, and the existing macro editorial bar still applies.

Distinguish three useful source roles without conflating them with factual authority:

- Attention: a guide notices something worth examining.
- Firsthand evidence: a participant, document, dataset, or original report establishes
  what happened.
- Interpretation: a knowledgeable person explains significance or points out a flaw.

One source can play several roles. Being good at interpretation does not make every
claim it makes primary evidence. Being an advocate does not make its original filing or
firsthand observation useless either.

The effective input mix needs an inventory before adding accounts. The static feeds and
September 6 X List snapshot lean toward institutions, markets, policy, and crypto trade
publications. A recognized source in `source_tiers.toml` is not necessarily a source we
actively collect.

Candidate discovery cohort, to deduplicate and verify before subscribing:

- HRF Financial Freedom reporting and Alex Gladstein for global financial repression,
  actual Bitcoin use, and the people affected. HRF publishes reports and works directly
  with activists through its [Financial Freedom program](https://hrf.org/program/financial-freedom/).
- Coin Center for original legal analysis, litigation, and financial privacy. Its
  [published work](https://coincenter.org/) provides a different view from routine crypto
  regulatory announcements. The owner has also approved its
  [Peer-to-Peer publication](https://p2p.coincenter.org/) for the planned intake.
- EFF for selected privacy and surveillance developments, not its entire technology feed;
  start with the [privacy program](https://www.eff.org/issues/privacy).
- Bitcoin Policy UK/Susie Ward for an additional jurisdiction and energy-policy lens;
  confirm the best collection surface using its [team](https://bitcoinpolicy.uk/our-team)
  and [mining/energy work](https://bitcoinpolicy.uk/mining-%26-energy).
- A few practitioner/reporting voices identified in the KB—such as Frank Corva, Jameson
  Lopp, calle, or Francis Pouliot—selected for gaps and original signal after examining
  effective current membership and a recent sample. This is a research shortlist, not
  a blanket recommendation to ingest every post from everyone named.

Some valuable context is in replies and quoted originals. Our general account queries
exclude replies. I would first strengthen selective follow-through from an eligible tip
to its original conversation, not ingest everybody's replies into the mailroom.

## 6. Use Perception to explore a question, not only find “Bitcoin”

Current collection uses a `bitcoin` feed keyword. The scheduled survey rotates through
self-custody, mining pools, merchant payments, and Bitcoin regulation. Those are useful
but narrower than the reader interests above.

Plan a small set of purpose-specific searches around financial access/capital controls,
privacy/self-custody rights, actual Bitcoin use, consequential monetary conditions, and
energy/open-technology developments. These are conceptual search briefs—not invented
Perception subject IDs or assumed Boolean syntax. Validate them against the provider's
taxonomy and endpoint contracts before implementation.

Perception's [API reference](https://perception.to/api/reference) documents regulatory,
coverage, regional/language, narrative, and voice-search capabilities. A useful further
experiment is tracing a reported speech or interview to the relevant voice segment;
the current writer tool set does not expose all of those capabilities.

Use narrative momentum as a pointer to what deserves a look, not as proof or a
popularity-based publication rule. Prefer targeted coverage gaps and active questions
over merely increasing calls. Reuse the current cache, quotas, provenance, and research
artifacts; no new tool suite is implied by this proposal.

HRF's [AI for Individual Rights program](https://hrf.org/program/ai-for-individual-rights/)
is a concrete example of the privacy/open-AI intersection worth investigating. That does
not mean generic AI industry news belongs on NBN.

## 7. Let posts earn different amounts of space and different media

Keep one wire, with flexible treatments rather than new compulsory products:

- A fast, concise report for a simple meaningful development.
- A short explanation when a confusing consequence is the actual value.
- An occasional timely synthesis when several verified developments form a genuinely
  useful new picture.

No return to mandatory Block threads, daily topic quotas, or manufactured evergreen posts.
Longer copy must earn its space. Use existing visual tools where a chart, document crop,
or genuinely relevant image makes the takeaway easier to understand at a glance. A chart
can replace a dense paragraph; a decorative image should not delay the post.

## 8. Measure editorial blind spots, not just processing success

Extend the audit's questions after approval:

- Which genuinely fitting stories did we miss, including outside the guide-account group?
- Was the failure collection, routing, research, freshness, framing, or delivery?
- Are we spotting real developments outside US institutions and market statistics when
  such opportunities exist? Topic counts alone cannot establish an editorial failure.
- Does the submitted copy explain the important consequence, or merely paraphrase a tip?
- Are we following unresolved stories through to outcomes and preserving corrections?
- Which added sources and queries contributed unique qualified leads, earlier evidence,
  or better context? Repeated coverage can help verification even when discovery is not unique.
- What did that contribution cost in research calls and time to a good draft?

Owner judgments are our strongest calibration evidence at current audience size. Likes
and reposts can remain context, not a training target that encourages outrage or repetition.

## A bounded first sprint

I would package the following for owner review before the independent lead-coder cycle:

1. A replacement audience/scope brief and a compact calibration set. Start with roughly
   12–20 positive, negative, and borderline cases from real owner feedback and a few
   explicitly hypothetical expanded-scope examples. Keep some cases held out from prompts.
2. Align active intake, preparation, writer, editor, and audit instructions to that brief.
   Replay the calibration cases at each relevant seat and inspect actual resulting copy,
   not just routing labels. Preserve ordinary editorial discretion and existing safety
   invariants. Model outputs are evidence, not a guaranteed deterministic pass/fail oracle.
3. Inventory effective sources, then pilot a small deduplicated cohort and a few validated
   Perception query replacements within existing budgets. Verify account identity and
   usable feed/API surfaces before turning anything on.
4. Use existing storylines to pilot a handful of reporting questions, initially with audit
   assistance. Review usefulness before authorizing autonomous scheduled follow-up logic.
5. Observe a representative week: fitting misses, unique/earlier source contribution,
   time to useful draft, owner assessment, and research cost. No forced content-volume or
   topic-mix target. Decide what to retain or remove based on results.

Model roster, publishing policy, and cadence need not change to test this. Keep autopost
off unless the owner separately enables it. Coordinate any later implementation with the
separate audit lane under the established pause/build/deploy/smoke/resume routine.

## Deliberately not proposing now

- Copying the entire KB into every run or adding a live production dependency on The Practice.
- Building another knowledge graph, a new multi-agent hierarchy, or a large source taxonomy.
- Treating KB conclusions as source verification or importing unrelated Swan brand rules.
- Broad general politics/AI coverage justified by a tenuous connection to Bitcoin.
- More elaborate gates, required research rituals, or forced original analysis per post.
- Increasing cadence, model effort, or research spend before measuring these changes.

The goal is a better-informed editor with a clearer sense of what to watch—not a larger
machine that makes it harder to publish a good story.
