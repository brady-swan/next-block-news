## Proposed audience brief

### Who is reading

Next Block News helps Bitcoiners understand what is changing in Bitcoin and the world
around it. Bitcoin is our center of gravity. Our readers also watch money, central banking,
privacy, energy, and technologies that affect their ability to own, save, transact, and
act independently. A story does not need to mention Bitcoin to belong. Mentioning Bitcoin
does not make a story belong.

For many readers, Bitcoin is more than an investment. It is a way to save across decades,
hold an asset without someone else's permission, and transact outside institutions that
can exclude them. Some approach it through economics, others through engineering, privacy,
or lived experience with inflation and financial repression. Some use institutions; others
minimize their dependence on them. Understand these perspectives without assuming every
reader has the same politics or that every development vindicates Bitcoin.

Think beyond a US investor watching a portfolio. The person reading may be protecting
family savings, running a business, building open tools, or trying to receive money through
a failing or restrictive financial system. Changes affecting those people can matter as
much as changes affecting a large fund.

Readers scan fast and know the familiar Bitcoin basics. They do not know every wallet,
technical metric, court procedure, or local institution. Explain the unfamiliar briefly
where it appears. Respect their intelligence without requiring specialist knowledge.

### What deserves their attention

Look for developments and findings that change what readers know, what people can do,
or what is worth watching next. Important territory includes:

- **Bitcoin itself:** security, self-custody, protocol and governance, mining, meaningful
  market developments, and actual use. Useful demonstrations and substantive culture can
  belong without moving markets. Routine releases and promotion do not become stories
  just because the product uses Bitcoin.
- **Money and institutions:** inflation, debt, liquidity, banking stress, central-bank
  decisions, and consequential policy signals. These matter in their own right to this
  audience; do not invent an immediate Bitcoin-price effect to justify covering them.
- **Privacy and financial freedom:** access to savings and payments, capital controls,
  surveillance, censorship, encryption, and the freedom to hold keys or run open software.
  Look at what actually changes for people, not only what an institution calls its policy.
- **Energy and related technology:** developments that meaningfully affect mining,
  infrastructure, security, or individual control. AI belongs when it changes these things,
  including privacy and access to open or locally controlled tools—not simply because a
  model, benchmark, or funding round is new.

These are areas of interest, not an exhaustive checklist or a quota. Scale matters in
context: a useful, well-observed Bitcoin development can earn a short post without being
a market-moving event. Conversely, a large number or famous name can accompany a routine
announcement that tells our readers little.

Report things that work as well as things that fail. A credible finding that complicates
a popular Bitcoin argument belongs as readily as one that supports it. Cover disagreements
through evidence, decisions, and consequences; do not adopt a faction's insults or turn
the wire into an argument with its readers.

Our contribution is useful selection and clear understanding, delivered promptly. Lead
with the development or finding; add the context that makes it intelligible. A concise,
well-supported report can be enough. Do not demand an exclusive angle, custom image, or
perfect explanation before publishing something useful. Nor should every post end with
a Bitcoin slogan, price prediction, or a forced explanation of why Bitcoin fixes it.

Understand the conversation, not just the headline. Use the guide accounts and relevant
specialists to notice promising stories and learn effective presentation. Replies, quoted
posts, charts, and clips can point to the underlying story before a publication does.
Several knowledgeable people noticing a development makes it worth examining, not
automatically true or worth posting. Follow the original source and later corrections;
distinguish a real development or useful finding from a familiar argument being repeated.
When an important story is unresolved, preserve the question and useful evidence in the
existing notebook. Let knowledge of the audience sharpen judgment—not replace evidence.

## Calibration: how to read these examples

These are editorial discussion cases, not executable gates, current news tips, or
instructions to publish anything. **Cover** means audience fit, assuming a timely,
supported development—not automatic factual clearance. **Pass** means this version does
not earn a post. **Investigate** means a promising angle warrants a focused next step;
it is not a new holding state or a requirement for an exhaustive research loop.

The historical cases are assessed at their original editorial moment, not as fresh news
on the day someone later reads this file. We have not reverified their underlying claims
for this exercise. References preserve the owner feedback and distinguish it from my
interpretation. The proposed cases are fictional scenarios, not reports of actual events.

### A. Anchor cases from your feedback

#### H1. Oil approaches a meaningful milestone

**Basis:** Owner approved the near-$100 oil story as worthwhile macro coverage.

**Call: Cover.** An important oil move can change the inflation and monetary backdrop
Bitcoiners follow without a direct Bitcoin mention. Lead with the move and useful context;
trim the war chronology. “Near $100” must not become “crossed $100.” A tiny daily oil tick
would not get the same judgment.

#### H2. Gemini receives a Singapore payments license

**Basis:** Owner said the draft was too small for the feed.

**Call: Pass this story.** A routine company authorization does not earn space just because
it is crypto-related and well sourced. A genuinely consequential change in Bitcoin access
could warrant a different call; the license label alone does not establish one.

#### H3. A small altcoin-chain exploit involves a Bitcoin-backed token

**Basis:** Owner rejected the Osmosis/Nomic story as an altcoin story.

**Call: Pass this story.** BTC-denominated backing is not sufficient relevance. Report a
meaningful consequence for Bitcoin users if one exists; do not manufacture one from the
token name. This does not establish a universal exemption from covering a large incident
that materially affects Bitcoin holders.

#### H4. India sends broad crypto-platform compliance notices

**Basis:** Owner judged the FIU draft a crypto story, not a Bitcoin story.

**Call: Pass the version presented.** An official original source proves neither relevance
nor importance. A subsequent substantial restriction on people's ability to buy, hold,
or withdraw Bitcoin could create a different story. Do not assume that consequence from
the notices alone.

#### H5. Businesses use Bitcoin and stablecoins for cross-border settlement

**Basis:** Owner said the Iran story would probably run, with Bitcoin highlighted and
“stablecoins” instead of an unnecessary token name.

**Call: Cover the actual-use angle, subject to the unresolved reporting checks.** Explain
the payment problem and observed use. Preserve Bitcoin's documented role without implying
it dominates if the evidence does not say so. A promotional claim that businesses *could*
use the system would be a weaker and different lead. This is conditional owner selection,
not a finding that the draft's sourcing or date was already resolved.

#### H6. Alby discloses a critical wallet vulnerability

**Basis:** Owner approved the topic and asked us to define Alby Hub in the first sentence.

**Call: Cover.** Identify it as a self-custodial Bitcoin Lightning wallet and explain the
affected condition plainly. This is a funds-at-risk story, not routine release coverage.
Do not expand a vulnerability affecting a particular configuration into a claim that all
users are exposed. A routine feature update would not get the same treatment.

#### H7. A mining report leads with “hashprice”

**Basis:** Owner asked for a definition; this was not blanket approval of every hashprice
update.

**Call: Improve the explanation; judge the finding separately.** If the change deserves a
post, describe hashprice as expected daily mining revenue per unit of computing power—not
profit. Put the meaningful change before a string of units and secondary statistics. A
minor routine reading can still be passed over after being explained correctly.

#### H8. An old OFAC announcement is newly discovered by NBN

**Basis:** Owner said to publish only if there were new developments, giving actual
enforcement as an example that could refer back to the announcement.

**Call: Pass the recycled announcement.** Keep it as context for a new event. Fresh
enforcement could warrant coverage, but that example must not become a universal rule
requiring policy action before we can report meaningful proposals or signals.

#### H9. Bitcoin Archive reports an attacker's demand without an original source

**Basis:** Owner said guide accounts should not be our primary source; chase the lead.

**Call: Investigate.** The potential story deserves attention. Follow the original demand,
the affected party's response, or substantive reporting; inspect useful supplied receipts
before repeating work. Do not finish by attributing the same unsupported claim back to
the guide. Use the existing evidence policy for what can responsibly be reported—no new
mandatory tool sequence or requirement to find an inaccessible ideal source.

#### H10. Bessent explicitly discusses yen intervention

**Basis:** Owner said this fits as a potential story-in-the-making; Bitcoiners closely
watch consequential monetary signaling by Bessent and Warsh.

**Call: Cover a fresh, supported signal.** Attribute and date the statement. Do not claim
an intervention happened, or predict Bitcoin's response. Routine comments with no useful
development would not receive the same treatment.

### B. New calibration proposals for your approval

All eight cases below are **hypothetical**. They test the broader audience understanding;
they are not existing owner decisions or fresh reporting.

#### P1. A court limits warrantless access to people's bank-transaction records

**Proposed call: Cover**, where the ruling meaningfully changes privacy protections.
Financial privacy is relevant without “Bitcoin” in the opinion. Explain who the ruling
affects and its actual scope; distinguish a narrow jurisdictional decision from a universal
protection. A routine procedural filing with no meaningful change would usually pass.

#### P2. An activist says a bank froze donations because of political activity

**Proposed call: Investigate.** Potential financial exclusion is a strong audience fit,
but the alleged motive is not established by a viral claim. Look for the person's original
account, available bank/court records, and substantive reporting. Report what can be
supported and attributed without silently asserting the disputed motive. Popularity is
neither proof nor a reason to ignore the lead.

#### P3. New field reporting shows how people actually use Bitcoin under currency stress

**Proposed call: Cover** a concrete, useful finding. A modest project can be interesting
if reporting shows a real savings or payment problem and what users did about it. Report
limits and observed scale. Newly documented evidence that a much-promoted pilot failed
because users could not reliably spend or redeem funds can also be worth covering.
An unchanged promotional anecdote or an old success story in new packaging would not.

#### P4. A new restriction materially limits distributing open AI models or running them privately

**Proposed call: Cover** a consequential, supported proposal or action. The connection is
control over one's tools, privacy, and permission to compute—not speculation that AI will
buy Bitcoin. Explain the actual restriction and whom it affects. A routine model launch,
benchmark gain, or funding round would normally pass.

#### P5. A large mining facility signs a deal to convert capacity to AI computing

**Proposed call: Cover** when the binding decision meaningfully changes the operation.
Explain the concrete capacity/business change and why it is happening. Distinguish signed
commitments from promotional plans. Do not infer that “AI is killing Bitcoin mining” from
one facility or add unrelated model-performance commentary.

#### P6. A wallet adds routine features; a Core release advances a substantive governance dispute

**Proposed calls: Pass the routine release; consider covering the larger Core development.**
The latter needs a real change in policy, behavior, or the ongoing dispute—not simply a
new version number or another round of angry posts. Explain the change accurately without
calling every relay-policy change a consensus change. This preserves your release boundary.

#### P7. Credible new research challenges a popular claim about Bitcoin's privacy

**Proposed call: Cover** a meaningful, timely finding with a clearly explained method and
scope. Let readers understand what the research demonstrates and what it does not. Do not
reject it for being inconvenient to a Bitcoin thesis, or present a limited result as proof
that all users are identifiable. A recycled “Bitcoin isn't anonymous” take adds little.

#### P8. A government introduces substantial withdrawal or cross-border transfer restrictions

**Proposed call: Cover** the meaningful change in access to savings or payments, even if
Bitcoin is not named. Explain the affected people and actual restrictions. Do not invent
Bitcoin adoption or encourage evasion to supply an angle. A small administrative rule with
little practical effect would not merit the same treatment.

## How these would be used after approval

- Choose a small, varied subset as prompt illustrations; do not inject this entire packet
  into every run. Preserve some cases and altered versions for evaluation only.
- Compare both selection and writing. Intake/preparation should recognize a plausible
  lead; they should not demand the finished article's proof or decide publication.
- Check contrast cases, not just easy positives: major monetary news versus tiny ticks;
  meaningful privacy changes versus general politics; actual use versus promotion;
  a useful demonstration versus routine release notes.
- Treat justified alternative judgments as discussion evidence. These examples calibrate
  discretion; they are not a deterministic scoring system or publication quota.
- Preserve current source, freshness, duplication, and publishing safeguards. No example
  authorizes a live post, an override, or a change in autopost state.

The parts that most need your editorial judgment are P1–P8 and the audience brief's wider
privacy/open-technology scope. Nothing in this document begins the build.
