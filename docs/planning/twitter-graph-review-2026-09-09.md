## Conclusion

The graph reinforces the proposed brief: Bitcoin is the center, but money, privacy,
institutional power, energy, open tools, and practical individual agency are part of the
audience's field of vision. It adds a particularly useful discovery lesson:

**Watch what knowledgeable people point to, not only the original posts they write.**

The most valuable adaptation is a small, role-aware expert network that can lead NBN to
an original statement, measurement, document, correction, or useful finding. It is not
another large context dump, a popularity-based publishing rule, or a second knowledge-graph
architecture. Preserve the existing news-wire guides and complement them with this network.

## What is particularly good about this graph

### 1. It remembers positions, not just topics

“These people are discussing custody” is a weak memory. “The initial interpretation was X;
the maintainer later narrowed it to Y; this question remains unresolved” is useful to a
writer. That makes a genuine development recognizable and prevents every session from
rediscovering the same debate.

NBN already has storylines, notebooks, and retrieval tools. The opportunity is to improve
what those memories retain: the current best understanding, its date, important corrections,
and what would constitute a new development. No need to import this entire graph into each run.

### 2. It distinguishes attention from argument quality

The roster's `R`/`T` distinction is useful: some people contribute careful reasoning; others
are valuable because they notice important topics. These roles should not collapse into a
single trust score. A person can also occupy different roles on different subjects.

For NBN, think in terms of **guide, specialist, original actor, and contextual voice**.
A specialist is not globally authoritative. An organization is an original source for its
own statement, not automatically for every claim it makes about the wider world.

This is descriptive context for model judgment, not four new deterministic editorial gates.

### 3. Its discovery mechanism follows outward links

`live/crawl.py` follows replies, quotes, reposts, and links to other X posts, then ranks
the target posts by the number of distinct roster accounts touching them. It is currently
an exact-post measure, not semantic event clustering.

The saved September 8 catch-up crawl gives concrete examples:

- An original `@Liquid_BTC` incident statement was touched by nine roster accounts.
- `@mempool` measurement and `@side_swap` statements were each touched by three.
- An `@OpenSats` report was touched by three despite substantially less engagement.
- A Seinfeld montage was touched by four, and a routine Capital B purchase appeared too.

These are observations from the saved crawl, **not proof of an NBN miss or a speed advantage**.
The crawl covered a multi-day catch-up window. The contrast is useful nevertheless: the
network can find original evidence and interesting noise alike. Judgment still has work to do.

Several people pointing at something should increase research priority, not count as
independent corroboration. Replies may be criticism. Several people may share an employer,
repeat one source, or belong to the same conversation cluster. A single good primary source
can justify a prompt short report without waiting for a convergence threshold or more likes.

## The concrete mismatch with NBN today

The account-search queries in `nbn/sources.py` exclude replies and reposts, including the
queries assembled from X-list membership. That is sensible for a compact headline stream,
but it leaves out the behavior this graph deliberately uses for discovery.

NBN already captures long-post text, quoted-post material, links, and media through
`nbn/lead_material.py`; this is **not** a recommendation to rebuild that completed work.
The missing capability is looking through selected experts' interactions for useful targets
and corrections, including targets outside the preselected account list.

The KB also records a multi-handle search failing to return one account's posts. That is a
reason to compare search with a small direct-timeline control, not evidence that NBN's
current queries have the same failure. The KB's own single-page timeline pulls are not a
lossless ingestion specification either. Any later implementation must measure pagination,
latency, deduplication, and API cost rather than copying the script wholesale.

## The small refinement I would make to the brief

Keep the approved direction. Refine its final guide-account paragraph rather than append
another long section:

> Understand the conversation, not just the headline. Use the guide accounts and relevant
> specialists to notice promising stories and learn effective presentation. Replies, quoted
> posts, charts, and clips can point to the underlying story before a publication does.
> Several knowledgeable people noticing a development makes it worth examining, not
> automatically true or worth posting. Follow the original source and later corrections;
> distinguish a real development or useful finding from a familiar argument being repeated.
> When an important story is unresolved, preserve the question and useful evidence in the
> existing notebook. Let knowledge of the audience sharpen judgment—not replace evidence.

The owner approved this refinement; it is incorporated into the audience-brief review document,
not the live prompt. Do not stuff the account list or collection implementation into the orientation.

### Additional calibration cases to consider

These are illustrative situations, not new factual reports or publication instructions.

1. **Several trusted accounts share one incident advisory.** Investigate promptly and read
   the advisory. The shares establish attention, not several independent confirmations.
   A useful supported short post need not wait for another publication.
2. **A maintainer narrows an alarming claim in a reply.** Read it and reflect the supported
   scope. Don't repeat the amplified version or automatically accept the maintainer's view
   of every disputed issue; distinguish what is known from interpretation.
3. **A small original account provides a useful measurement or documented change.** Consider
   it on substance. Zero likes is not a reason to pass.
4. **Several large accounts resume a familiar factional argument.** Awareness may be enough.
   A real decision, finding, changed position with consequences, or useful new explanation
   can earn coverage; argument volume by itself does not.
5. **A quoted chart appears to prove a popular thesis.** Inspect the actual series and date.
   A chart about general construction cannot silently become evidence about Bitcoin mining.

## Memory and freshness: borrow the discipline, not the whole system

Keep three things separate:

- **Durable audience understanding:** the orientation brief.
- **Dated conversation context:** what people thought, what changed, what remains open.
- **Current reporting evidence:** the sources supporting this particular post.

The graph itself warns that a `hot` label can outlive its evidence. Several nodes were last
read in July, and a recent sweep/render is not proof that their arguments were revisited.
The September 8 sweep note explicitly distinguishes a fresh machine pull from updating
the thread files. An old open-question section can also survive after a later correction.

For NBN memory, preserve an as-of/check date and the correction with the finding. Do not
equate retrieval time, file mtime, or a fresh article with a fresh underlying event.
Avoid turning an analyst's hypothesis into a fact merely because it has a stable node name.

Finally, this KB was built partly for Swan work. Its marketing-specific output fences and
its historical source exclusions are context about that system, not new NBN policy. Where
they conflict with the owner's explicit NBN decisions, do not import them. Reading a
viewpoint need not mean adopting it or amplifying its unrelated claims.
