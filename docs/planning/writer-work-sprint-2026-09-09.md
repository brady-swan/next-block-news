## Outcome we want

A fresh Writer session should understand the audience, receive worthwhile leads and due
follow-ups, know what its predecessor thought mattered, retrieve earlier work without guessing
its wording, and leave the next shift a useful handoff. This should improve timely, clear,
well-sourced posts without creating pressure to post when there is no worthwhile development.

## 3. Ongoing stories become a reporting agenda

The [current-behavior investigation](story-follow-up-review-2026-09-09.md) found usable
event notebooks, optional storyline `watch_for` notes, but no mechanism that makes those notes
due reporting assignments. At the approximately 17:47 UTC production check, several Liquid
event notebooks existed but no Liquid storyline did. This is dated evidence, not a permanent
assertion about production state.

Approved follow-up direction for a developing story:

- Last established understanding and what readers have actually seen, with dates.
- The open question or observable development worth checking next.
- The most useful original source paths and previous reporting artifacts.
- A sensible next-check time or expected milestone, plus when attention should taper or end.

Bring due follow-ups into an ordinary desk even if no new feed item mentions the subject.
Use the existing desk cadence; the Writer nominates the reporting question and appropriate
next check. The letter preserves the reasoning; durable follow-up state makes the check due.
Neither requires every observation in a letter to become another research assignment.
Do not confuse deferred unfinished work with a follow-up on a successfully delivered story.
A check may lead to a new post, an improvement to an unpublished draft, a memory-only update,
or closure. A no-change result is useful and should not force copy or constant repeated searches.

Design still needed: how Writer judgment nominates a follow-up; minimal durable scheduling
state; how prep sees due work; how no-change checks update a storyline without inventing an
incoming article; collision handling with fresh related leads; and reasonable bounded attention.
No final intervals, caps, or schema selected yet.

## 4. A letter to the next shift

Every completed Writer session must leave a useful, freely written letter while it still
has the context. This is part of finishing the job, not an optional “anything else?” field.
Capture what mattered, what changed its understanding, useful reporting paths, what went
nowhere, and where it would look next. It should be selective, not a transcription of tool
calls or a second exhaustive dossier. No minimum word count or pressure to invent a lesson.

No-post sessions still have a handoff: what was considered and why it did not advance, what
was checked with no change, a correction to inherited context, or the specific question still
worth watching. Do not offer “nothing to add” as the routine easy exit. A short concrete note
is sufficient; generic reassurance, repeating an old letter, or manufacturing new research
to fill space is not. A scheduled cycle in which no Writer runs has no new Writer letter;
do not fabricate one or invoke a model solely to fill that gap.

The next Writer should see the latest useful handoff prominently. Older letters can remain
retrievable. The successor should revise or retire old assumptions, not endlessly reproduce
the previous letter. A new date alone does not make inherited observations current.

Letter contract and remaining lifecycle details:

- Capture the required letter with the Writer's normal final submission rather than adding
  a separate model turn by default.
- Label its viewpoint/time. The Writer may finish before the Editor and delivery complete.
  Actual subsequent Editor and publication outcomes must accompany or qualify it, without
  pretending the Writer witnessed those later outcomes.
- Missing letters are an observable incomplete handoff, not an accepted editorial choice.
  Define bounded technical recovery during review; preserve otherwise useful work rather than
  silently losing copy or fabricating a replacement letter after a crash. Link letters to
  their run/relevant memory and prevent duplicate writes when resuming a completed operation.
- Set a sensible context-size bound and retain older letters within NBN's memory lifecycle.
  Avoid turning a letter into a permanent command or another authority layer.
- Letters guide attention; they do not replace original sources, event identity, or due-work
  scheduling. The personal agent's existing letters have not been newly inspected for this design.

## 5. Meaning-based search — lightweight baseline; QMD in reserve

Current `search_memory` and `search_intake` use ANDed literal substring terms. They do not
perform vector retrieval. The prep model's selection from a bounded storyline index is a
different capability from searching the reporting archive semantically.

### Aragorn reference inspected, September 9

Read all 206 lines of the supplied
[aragorn-recall.mjs](/Users/brady/Downloads/aragorn-recall.mjs) without executing it, opening
the personal index, or accessing its corpus. It implements paragraph-aware chunks, local
`nomic-embed-text` embeddings with distinct query/document prefixes, cached vectors keyed by
model and content hash, linear cosine ranking, and best-chunk-per-document results. Keyword
mode ranks partial term/phrase matches directly from documents without an embedding index.
The owner's 5,348-chunk / 84 MB size and few-hundred-millisecond timing are reported figures,
not measurements from this review.

Useful design choices to borrow:

- Explicitly select NBN record types; do not crawl private personal material or configuration.
- Index substantive bodies rather than navigation catalogs. Curated storyline summaries and
  shift letters are substantive reporting context, not automatically excluded as “summaries.”
- Re-embed only changed chunks; invalidate the cache when the embedding specification or
  chunking changes. Keep the model-appropriate query/document task prefixes.
- Return varied documents rather than eight adjacent chunks from one long record. Let the
  Writer open more context; do not collapse an entire storyline and hide differing evidence.
- Preserve useful keyword retrieval when embeddings are unavailable.

Implementation nuances: the supplied CLI defaults to keyword mode; semantic mode is opt-in.
Semantic failures exit rather than automatically falling back, so the gateway would need to
provide that behavior (its code was not inspected). Graph-directory scans are flat, while
the general text/chatlog walker is recursive. “No service” means no separate retrieval service
or vector database: Ollama still serves the embedding model. These distinctions do not negate
the simple design; they clarify what NBN would actually need to implement and host.

### Selected inference direction: Ollama + nomic-embed-text

The owner confirmed these are the two inference components in the personal setup: a reported
66 MB Ollama binary and a 274 MB downloaded model. The local binary was not inspected or copied.
For Railway use a pinned Linux build/container, not the Mac executable. Ollama documents
[CPU-only Docker operation](https://docs.ollama.com/docker); its
[model listing](https://ollama.com/library/nomic-embed-text) confirms the 274 MB download.
Railway supports [Dockerfile services](https://docs.railway.com/builds/dockerfiles).
This makes the pairing a concrete, feasible baseline—not an unproven need for GPU infrastructure.

Proposed packaging: an internal-only embedding endpoint, a persisted model cache, and NBN's
existing SQLite-backed retrieval records. A private companion service is an option; settle
packaging during implementation review rather than committing to another service now. Cache
document embeddings and embed only new/changed text plus search queries. No paid model API
or additional writing model is required for this route.

Download size is not resident RAM. Measure actual memory, CPU and query/indexing latency;
these are sizing checks, not a reason to defer this approach indefinitely. Railway's published
[resource rates](https://docs.railway.com/pricing/plans) on September 9 are $10/GB-month RAM,
$20/vCPU-month CPU and $0.15/GB-month volume storage. For illustration, 0.5 GB continuously
resident would be about $5/month in RAM usage alone; this is not a measured NBN footprint
or total-cost forecast. Local embedding removes token fees, not hosting usage.

### QMD facts checked against upstream, September 9

[QMD's README](https://github.com/tobi/qmd) describes BM25 keyword search, semantic vectors,
query expansion, and model reranking through node-llama-cpp. It offers CLI, SDK, MCP, and HTTP
interfaces. Defaults include EmbeddingGemma, a Qwen reranker, and a query-expansion model;
“llama” is the runtime connection, not one Llama model doing every job. It supports collection
context and separate indexing/embedding operations. These upstream defaults are not a check
of the owner's installed configuration. Review covered relevant README sections, not a full
source audit or performance test.

### Proposed NBN fit — inference and design questions, not upstream promises

The owner agreed to the Aragorn-style baseline. Start with exact/keyword lookup plus semantic
ranking, without query expansion or another chat-model reranking step. Test against actual
retrieval cases; only investigate QMD's extra capabilities if a demonstrated gap warrants it.
Do not make a QMD installation or comparison implementation a dependency of this sprint.
Keep the Writer-facing interface simple: improve existing memory search and return concise
matches that can be opened with existing context tools. Neither match type may automatically
merge events, declare a duplicate, or certify a claim.

NBN's authoritative work already lives in SQLite. The lightweight baseline will cache chunk vectors
alongside those records and scan them at a measured small corpus size; a separate vector
database or a copy of the 84 MB JSON-file design is not inherently necessary. Keep stable NBN
artifact IDs, revisions, dates and record types. Use bounded embedding requests with an explicit
keyword fallback; make index updates atomic so retrieval cannot read half-written state.
If adopting QMD instead, design a rebuildable projection of selected notebooks, storylines,
source extracts, reporting notes and letters into its collections. Neither choice creates a
competing editable memory store or depends on the laptop's personal index in production.

Questions to settle through a small evaluation:

- Which records and chunks improve recall? Avoid filling search with repetitive raw tool JSON.
- Do exact, lexical, semantic, and combined searches find the right prior work on actual NBN queries?
- Which specific query failures, if any, justify later investigation of expansion or reranking?
- What are cold/warm latency, resident memory, indexing time, and hosting cost on the intended
  Railway runtime? Local inference is not zero infrastructure cost; laptop performance is not proof.
- How do updates, expired records, corrections, and deleted/replaced material leave the index?
- Can ordinary retrieval keep working if semantic search is unavailable or indexing is behind?
- Does the chosen lightweight backend satisfy held-out retrieval cases and operational needs?
  Set runtime sizing, pinned versions and process packaging from these checks; QMD stays in reserve.

Index new or changed material incrementally, not the entire archive every Writer run. Preserve
source dates and current editorial/publication state when opening a result. Start with NBN's
notebooks, research extracts, storylines and its own new shift letters in existing retention
windows, including open storylines. This does not approve importing the personal KB or letters.

## 6. Desk visibility and evaluation

Propose a compact way to inspect the handoff delivered to a Writer, active/due follow-up
questions, checks completed with or without a post, and retrieval that materially helped or
failed. Keep the approved run-centered Desk and its layout; this is not another redesign.

After approval, extend the separate audit's existing scope to ask:

- Are fitting stories and useful originals reaching the Writer sooner?
- Did the next shift act on a worthwhile handoff without inheriting stale assumptions?
- Did every completed Writer session leave a concrete useful letter, including no-post sessions?
  Flag missing or generic/copy-forward handoffs without rewarding verbosity or invented lessons.
- Did follow-ups find real developments that fresh-feed intake alone would not have supplied?
- Did letters and semantic matches save research or improve a post, rather than just add tokens?
- Are corrections and actual Editor/publication outcomes visible alongside recalled material?
- Does the final copy remain clear, useful, timely, and appropriately selective?

Measure retrieval quality, duplicate/false-association cases, no-change checks, source contribution,
time to a useful draft, and incremental model/hosting cost. Use representative historical cases
plus deliberately different wording and similar-but-distinct events. Assess quality with owner
review; do not equate a perfect tool call or semantic score with a good editorial decision.
