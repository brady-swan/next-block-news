# Next Block News — orientation examples

This is the human-curated staging ledger for examples that may later be incorporated into
the live newsroom orientation. Adding an entry here does **not** change the production
prompt. Review and promote examples deliberately so one-off reactions do not silently
become system policy.

Each entry should preserve the useful editorial core, identify the failure mode or drift,
and state the reusable lesson. Prefer real NBN work over invented examples.

## 2026-09-02 — Bitcoin/gold correlation: current condition, not trading signal

**Source candidate:** The Block, “Bitcoin and gold move in lockstep as debasement trade
gains more steam.” The original page could not be fetched directly; the desk inspected a
secondary page repeating the reported figures. Typefully draft `10593702` was subsequently
edited and published by Brady.

**Approved core framing:**

> Bitcoin’s correlation with gold has reached a record high as both assets benefit from
> the debasement trade.

**What made the story useful:** The record correlation is an observable Bitcoin market
condition. Connecting it to simultaneous demand for scarce assets explains why the data
matters to a Bitcoin reader without bolting on generic macro commentary.

**What the generated draft got wrong:** It moved from reporting the current relationship
into selected historical analogues about 172% and 350% Bitcoin rallies, then added a Fear &
Greed reading. That changed the genre from news and explanation into technical analysis and
an implied trading signal.

**Reusable lesson:** Use market data to explain present conditions and the monetary context.
Do not turn correlations, chart patterns, sentiment gauges, or prior cycles into forecasts,
price targets, rally analogues, or trading setups. Historical context belongs only when it
clarifies the reported development rather than suggesting what the reader should expect or
do next.

## 2026-09-02 — Capital B financing: below the treasury-company bar

**Published post:** [Capital B private placement](https://x.com/nextblocknews_/status/2095134649886691823),
linked to a generic Phemex news page.

**What the generated post did:** It devoted a long paragraph to a €7.6 million private
placement, share premium, four warrant tranches, potential additional financing, and a
possible 376 BTC purchase by Capital B.

**Owner judgment:** Do not post this. Capital B is outside the focused treasury-company set
and this financing does not clear the high bar for covering another treasury company. Adam
Back's participation and detailed deal mechanics do not make a routine potential Bitcoin buy
important to the reader.

**Reusable lesson:** Outside Strategy, Metaplanet, and Strive, treasury-company activity needs
an exceptional reason to exist on the feed: genuine market consequence, a major household
name, a category-changing first, or another development that materially changes the reader's
picture. Financing terms and a possible future purchase by a small issuer are not enough.
Apply the importance test before researching or polishing the transaction details.

## 2026-09-02 — Ireland savings wrapper: right story, overwritten copy

**Published post:** [Ireland's tax-advantaged savings account](https://x.com/nextblocknews_/status/2095137919740887401),
linked to Brefcrypto.

**What made the story useful:** Ireland designed a new tax-advantaged investment account that
can hold listed securities and funds while excluding direct Bitcoin ownership. That is a
concrete state-policy decision affecting Bitcoin's treatment, not merely another official
expressing skepticism about “crypto.” This is a definite post.

**What the generated post got wrong:** It overexplained the European Commission background,
UCITS eligibility, the distinction from a purchase ban, the unconfirmed treatment of a
Bitcoin-exposed ETF, and future budget details. Some context was useful, but the cumulative
detail buried a simple policy development. The long second paragraph also needed more blank
line breaks for an X reader.

**Reusable lesson:** Once the key policy distinction is clear, stop. Keep only the context
that changes the reader's understanding. Longer posts should still use short, scannable
paragraphs—usually one or two sentences—with blank lines between them. Story weight may
justify length; it does not justify a wall of text.

These two posts mark a healthy next phase: the desk is producing enough plausible work that
selection and editing quality, rather than a total lack of candidates, is now the problem to
solve.
